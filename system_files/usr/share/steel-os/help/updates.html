Updates and going back

Steel OS keeps itself up to date, and always keeps the version before.

How updates work

  • Every Tuesday morning, Steel OS downloads the new version in the background. If the computer was off, it does it at the next start.
  • The new version starts the next time you restart. Nothing changes while you are working. A notification says Restart to finish updating, with Restart now and Later.
  • If you don't restart, Steel OS restarts by itself at night, between 2 and 5 am, but only when nobody is using the computer (screen locked or untouched for 30 minutes), no game or OBS is running, and a laptop is plugged in. It warns 15 minutes first; Not now skips that night. To switch this off: Steel OS Portal, Welcome, Automatic restarts for updates.
  • Your apps update themselves too.

Update now

Open the Steel OS Portal, then Welcome, then Update now. A window shows what it is doing; no password and no terminal. When a new version is ready it offers Restart now.

"A new version is being published right now": you clicked Update now in the minute a new version was going out, before its signature had arrived. Nothing is wrong with your PC; click Try again, or leave it, and Steel OS updates by itself at night.

Already updating? If an update is running in the background (the Tuesday one starts when a laptop that slept through midnight wakes up), Update now simply shows that update's progress instead of starting a second one.

Going back to the previous version

If something looks wrong after an update:

  1. Restart the computer.
  2. When the menu appears at start-up, pick the second entry. That is the version you had before.
  3. Steel OS starts as it was. Your files, settings and apps are untouched.

Or from the Portal: Troubleshoot, then Roll back to the previous Steel OS version.

Which version am I on?

System Settings, About this System. The terminal (Super+Enter) shows it under its welcome message. For the full picture type steel-os-version: the version you are running, an update waiting for a restart, and the previous version kept in the start-up menu.

The weekly check-in

Once a week, and once on its very first start, your PC fetches a tiny file from steel-os.org. We count those fetches to see how many PCs use Steel OS. It sends no name, no account, no ID and nothing about your computer. To turn it off, open Konsole and type sudo systemctl disable --now steel-os-checkin.timer, then enter your password. Updates keep working.
